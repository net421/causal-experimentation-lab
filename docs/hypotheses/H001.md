# Hypothesis card H001 — gross margin

## Decision context

The intervention gives selected distribution centers priority in replenishment allocation for eight weeks. The decision is whether evidence is strong enough to invite a governed real-world pilot review.

## Population, treatment, comparison, outcome

- Population: 500 synthetic distribution centers across four regions.
- Treatment: priority replenishment.
- Comparison: business-as-usual allocation.
- Primary outcome: gross margin in US dollars over eight post-assignment weeks.
- Assignment: seeded complete randomization with exactly 250 centers per arm.

## Estimand and estimator

The estimand is the intention-to-treat average effect. The pre-specified estimator is the treatment coefficient from OLS ANCOVA, adjusting for baseline gross margin, baseline stockout rate, capacity index, and region. Inference uses HC1 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors and a two-sided large-sample normal 95% interval.

## Success thresholds

The adjusted estimate must be at least $250, its 95% interval must exclude zero, its two-sided p-value must not exceed 0.05, planned power must meet 0.80, all baseline covariate absolute standardized mean differences must not exceed 0.10, the randomization-inference p-value must not exceed 0.05, and every leave-one-region-out effect must be positive.

Passing the thresholds means only `eligible_for_human_review`. It does not authorize a pilot or rollout.

## Risks and interpretation

The dataset is synthetic. Therefore the result validates code, analysis discipline, and evidence packaging—not the business effectiveness of priority replenishment. A real test needs contamination controls, cost measurement, harm monitoring, and accountable approval.
